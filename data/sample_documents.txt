Vector databases are specialized systems designed to store and retrieve high-dimensional vectors efficiently. They power semantic search, recommendation engines, and retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) by enabling fast approximate nearest neighbor (ANN) search over millions of embeddings. Unlike traditional databases that match exact keys or text, vector databases find items that are semantically similar based on distance metrics such as cosine similarity or L2 distance.

Endee is a high-performance vector database built for production AI workloads. It offers fast ANN search using the HNSW algorithm, support for cosine, L2, and inner product spaces, and metadata filtering with operators like equality and range. Endee is designed for teams building real-world AI systems at scale, with a focus on high recall, low latency, strong security, and cost efficiency.

Retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) combines dense retrieval with large language models to improve answer quality and reduce hallucination. In a typical RAG pipeline, documents are chunked and embedded, then stored in a vector database. At query time, the user question is embedded and used to retrieve the most relevant chunks; those chunks are passed as context to an LLM, which generates an answer grounded in the retrieved content. This approach is widely used for enterprise search, question-answering over private data, and AI assistants.

Embeddings are dense vector representations of text, images, or other data. Sentence-transformers and similar models produce embeddings such that semantically similar inputs map to nearby vectors in the embedding space. Common embedding dimensions range from 384 (e.g. all-MiniLM-L6-v2) to 1536 (OpenAI text-embedding-ada-002). Choosing the right model involves trade-offs between quality, speed, and dimension size for your vector store.

Semantic search enables users to find content by meaning rather than exact keywords. A query like "How do I reset my password?" can match support articles about account recovery even when the exact phrase does not appear. This is achieved by embedding both the query and the corpus, then retrieving items with the smallest distance (or largest similarity) to the query vector. Vector databases like Endee are built to make this search fast at scale.
